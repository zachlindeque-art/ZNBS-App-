Before you donate

Plan for about an hour. The needle part takes around ten minutes. Staff check everyone before they give, and they have the final say.

Ways to donate

Whole blood

The most common donation. About 450 ml of blood is collected.

How often
Men every 3 months, women every 4 months
Takes
About 10 minutes with the needle
Helps
Mothers who bleed in childbirth, children with severe anaemia, and accident and surgery patients.

Platelets

A machine collects only your platelets and returns the rest of your blood to you. This is called apheresis.

How often
Every 2 weeks
Takes
1 to 2 hours
Helps
Platelets help blood to clot. They are used for patients with cancer, leukaemia and severe bleeding.

Plasma

Plasma is the liquid part of your blood. It is also collected by apheresis.

How often
Every 2 weeks
Takes
About 1 hour
Helps
Patients with severe burns, heavy bleeding, and liver or clotting problems.

Platelet and plasma donation may only be available at some centres. Ask your nearest centre.

Who can usually give

  • You are aged 16 to 65
  • You weigh at least 50 kg
  • You feel well on the day
  • You consider your blood safe to give to a patient

On arrival the team checks your weight, blood pressure and haemoglobin (iron level) with a quick finger prick.

Before you go

  • Eat a proper meal in the 3 to 4 hours before
  • In the days before, eat iron-rich food such as beans, kapenta, eggs, meat and dark green vegetables like rape or pumpkin leaves
  • Drink plenty of water
  • Avoid alcohol the day before
  • Carry your NRC or another photo ID
  • Wear a top with sleeves that roll up easily

After you donate

  • Rest for 10 to 15 minutes and take the drink and snack offered
  • Drink extra fluids for the next 24 hours
  • Skip heavy lifting and hard exercise for the rest of the day
  • If you feel faint, sit or lie down with your feet raised
  • Log the donation on your donor card so your reminder stays accurate

Check with the team first if you

  • Have a fever, cough or feel unwell
  • Have had malaria recently
  • Have had a vaccination recently
  • Are pregnant or have recently given birth
  • Had a tattoo, piercing, surgery or a transfusion recently
  • Take regular medication

About this app

Pulumutsa Moyo is an independent prototype, built for a pilot with the Zambia National Blood Transfusion Service (ZNBTS). It isn't an official ZNBTS app yet. The donation rules and health advice come from public sources, and ZNBTS staff will check them before launch. The pins for Lusaka and Kitwe sit on the hospital; the others mark the town. Everything you enter stays on this phone.
